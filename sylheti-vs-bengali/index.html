Sylheti vs Bengali: What's the Difference?

They are closely related, but a Sylheti speaker can miss a lot in formal Bengali. Here is what differs, with real examples, and how to choose the right translator.

By Shadman Sadekin, native Sylheti and Bengali translatorUpdated 30 October 2026

Short answer: Sylheti is the language of the Sylhet region of north-east Bangladesh. It is related to Bengali, but its sounds, everyday words and verb endings are different enough that many Sylheti speakers find formal Bengali hard to follow, especially in legal, medical or official settings. If the person you are communicating with grew up speaking Sylheti, ask for a Sylheti translator or interpreter.

Where Sylheti is spoken

Sylheti is spoken by more than 10 million people in the Sylhet Division of Bangladesh and in parts of north-east India, including the Barak Valley in Assam. It is also the main home language of most British Bangladeshi families, whose roots are largely in Sylhet, and of large communities in the United States and the Middle East.

Linguists treat Sylheti as its own language with its own code, syl, in the ISO 639-3 standard. In daily life many people call it a dialect of Bengali, and both views are common. What matters in practice is whether the person you are serving understands it better than standard Bengali.

How Sylheti sounds different

Several regular sound changes make Sylheti easy to recognise:

  • P becomes F. Bengali পানি (pani, water) is ফানি (fani) in Sylheti.
  • K becomes a breathy KH. Bengali করা (kora, to do) is খরা (khora).
  • H is often dropped. Bengali হয়েছে (hoyeche, has happened) is অইছে (oise).

How Sylheti is written

Sylheti has its own historic script, Syloti Nagri (ꠍꠤꠟꠐꠤ means "Sylheti"), which was added to Unicode in 2005. Today most Sylheti readers are more comfortable with Bengali script, spelled the way Sylheti is spoken, and younger people often write it in Roman letters on their phones.

There is no single official spelling, so a good Sylheti translation is written the way the reader actually speaks. For people who do not read at all, the best option is often a written translation plus a spoken read-through, known as sight translation.

Can Sylheti speakers understand Bengali?

It depends on the person. Many Sylheti speakers learned standard Bengali at school in Bangladesh and follow it well. Others, including many older people and people born in the UK, understand everyday Bengali partly but struggle with formal or technical language.

That gap matters most where precision matters: a witness statement, a care plan, a medical consent form or a benefits assessment. If someone says "yes" to a Bengali question they only half understood, the result can be unfair or unsafe.

Which one do you need?

  • Ask the person or their family which language they speak at home. Forms often say "Bengali" when the person actually speaks Sylheti.
  • Choose Sylheti for spoken interpreting, audio transcription and documents for people who grew up speaking Sylheti, especially in the UK diaspora.
  • Choose standard Bengali for readers educated in Bengali, official documents for use in Bangladesh, and audiences across Bangladesh and West Bengal.
